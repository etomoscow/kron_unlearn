\begin{abstract}
% Preference-based LLM unlearning methods such as NPO and SimNPO can reach useful \texttt{forget--retain} trade-offs, but they often spend their early optimization budget discovering a direction that separates forget-specific behavior from retained capability. We study whether a second-order initializer can supply that direction before iterative unlearning begins. K-FORGE estimates Kronecker-factored empirical Fishers on forget and retain data, simultaneously diagonalizes the two Fisher pairs, and applies a retain-aware Wiener edit in the resulting generalized-eigenvector basis. The unconstrained edit has an exact closed form; for low-rank edits with positive retain penalty, the exact weighted low-rank problem is not closed form, so we use a separable relaxation that is exact in the full-rank limit and at zero retain penalty. On TOFU \texttt{forget10} with Llama 3.2 1B, corrected one-shot K-FORGE traces a controllable Pareto frontier but does not dominate iterative baselines. Its main value is as initialization: at matched budgets of 50, 100, and 250 steps, K-FORGE-initialized NPO and SimNPO consistently lower forget probability relative to scratch training while preserving or improving utility. Corrected ablations show that retain awareness is necessary, while a diagonal control recovers part, but not all, of the effect. These results support K-FORGE as a curvature-aware initializer for preference-based unlearning rather than as a one-shot replacement for iterative methods.

Preference-based LLM unlearning methods such as NPO and SimNPO can reach useful \texttt{forget--retain} trade-offs, but early optimization must discover directions that suppress forget-set behavior without damaging retained capability. We ask whether this direction can be supplied by a closed-form second-order initializer. K-FORGE uses forget- and retain-set Fisher information to construct a closed-form low-rank edit, then uses the edited model as the starting point for NPO or SimNPO. On TOFU with Llama 3.2 models, one-shot K-FORGE produces a controllable forget--utility frontier. Using K-FORGE as an initializer improves matched-budget NPO and SimNPO runs, reducing forget probability with comparable utility preservation. Matched initialization controls show that the gain is not explained by an arbitrary low-rank perturbation, and experiments across different model sizes show that K-FORGE is a better curvature-aware starting point for a different preference-based LLM unlearning methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM unlearning is a task of selectively removing the influence of specific training data without retraining the given model from scratch~\citep{tofu,rmu,muse}. Current LLM unlearning methods cluster into two regimes: (i) iterative preference-based methods such as Negative Preference Optimization~\citep{npo} and its simplified variant SimNPO~\citep{simnpo} frame unlearning as a one-sided preference-optimization problem and require hundreds to thousands of fine-tuning steps to reach a reasonable forget–retain trade-off; (ii) representation-misdirection methods such as RMU~\citep{rmu} perturb intermediate activations and are highly effective but task-specific. In contrast, one-shot or closed-form methods — including selective synaptic dampening~\citep{foster24}, FILA~\citep{fila}, and Gauss–Newton style edits~\citep{kfade} — are cheap but tend to either under-forget or sharply damage retained capabilities.

This bimodality is not incidental. Iterative methods can course-correct: every gradient step re-linearizes the loss landscape around the current weights, so destructive directions can be discovered and avoided. One-shot methods solve a single quadratic surrogate and must commit to a magnitude before observing its consequences. As a result, scaling a one-shot edit usually produces a strength cliff: forget metrics improve monotonically with edit magnitude until utility collapses abruptly. We try to make one-shot methods useful without sacrificing their controllability.

We address this gap by treating Kronecker-Fisher edits as initializers rather than finished unlearning procedures. Our starting point is matrix-free Fisher factorization (MFF)~\cite{chekalina_2026_gfwsvd}, which provides GPU-tractable Kronecker factors for layerwise empirical Fishers. We estimate one Fisher on forget data and one on retain data, simultaneously diagonalize the two Fisher pairs, and derive a retain-aware Wiener edit in that basis. We call the resulting construction \textbf{K-FORGE} (\textbf{K}ronecker-\textbf{F}isher \textbf{O}ne-shot \textbf{R}ank-r \textbf{G}eneralized \textbf{E}rasure).

Our experiments support this initializer view. On TOFU \texttt{forget10} with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, one-shot K-FORGE traces a controllable forget--utility frontier, but does not by itself dominate iterative unlearning. When used as initialization, however, K-FORGE improves matched-budget NPO and SimNPO runs, reducing forget probability while preserving comparable utility. Matched initialization controls suggest that the gain is not explained by arbitrary low-rank perturbations alone. Additional TOFU splits and a Llama-3.2-3B sanity check provide early evidence that the effect is not limited to a single 1B setting, though broader benchmark and scaling evidence remains future work.

Our contributions are three-fold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce \textbf{K-FORGE}, a closed-form Fisher-guided initializer for preference-based LLM unlearning. K-FORGE uses separate forget and retain curvature estimates to construct a low-rank edit that gives NPO and SimNPO a more informative starting point than the original checkpoint.

    \item We derive a two-Fisher Wiener formulation that makes the role of retain awareness explicit. The full-rank solution is exact, and the practical low-rank case is handled by a stated relaxation rather than by  assuming an unavailable closed-form optimum.

    \item Our extensive experimentgs show that \textbf{K-FORGE} improves early preference-based unlearning on TOFU. At matched optimization budgets, initialized with \textbf{K-FORGE} NPO and SimNPO reduce forget probability while preserving comparable utility.
  \end{enumerate}
  
K-FORGE is complementary to existing unlearning methods: it changes the starting point, not the downstream preference objective. Code is released openly online\footnote{ \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/kforge-C710/}}.


\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{LLM unlearning methods.}
LLM unlearning methods differ in how they induce forgetting and how they preserve retained capabilities. Preference-based methods adapt DPO-style objectives to unlearning: NPO~\citep{npo} uses a reference-model preference term, SimNPO~\citep{simnpo} removes the reference model. RMU~\citep{rmu} modifies intermediate activations. Gradient-ascent, gradient-difference, task-vector, and inverse-DPO variants provide additional baselines. TOFU~\citep{tofu}, MUSE~\citep{muse}, WMDP~\citep{rmu}, and OpenUnlearning~\citep{openunlearning} define much of the current empirical landscape. Our work does not introduce a new preference loss; instead, it studies whether a closed-form curvature edit can improve the initialization of existing preference-based methods.

\paragraph{Fisher-based and second-order unlearning.}
Fisher information~\citep{fisher1922} by definition estimates the parameters which are important to forgetting and retention. Methods such as Selective Synaptic Dampening~\citep{foster24} use only the main diagonal of the Fisher Information matrix and scale parameters using per-weight forget-vs-retain importance. FILA~\citep{fila} and VILA~\citep{vila} use the Fisher information to improve unlearning. SOUL~\citep{soul} uses Sophia-style second-order (and thus providing richer information) optimizer~\citep{sophia}, K-FADE~\citep{kfade} applies Kronecker-factored Gauss--Newton updates, and WIN-U~\citep{winu} uses Woodbury-scaled Newton updates without retain-set access. Ours \textbf{K-FORGE} is different. We use two Fisher estimates to construct a closed-form low-rank edit and use this edit as an initializer for preference optimization rather than as the full unlearning procedure.

\paragraph{Fisher Information approximations.}
K-FAC~\citep{martens2015optimizing} and EK-FAC~\citep{exfac} approximate layer-wise Fisher information with Kronecker factors and thus make the estimation of almost-exact Fisher Information matrix feasible. Kronecker-Fisher approximations have also been used for
influence functions at LLM scale~\citep{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2308-03296} and for compression via matrix-free Fisher factorization and GFWSVD~\citep{chekalina_2026_gfwsvd}. K-FORGE builds on this line by estimating separate forget and retain Fishers and using their contrast to choose unlearning directions.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

Let $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ denote the weight matrix of a single linear layer in a pretrained model with parameters $\theta^\star$. We denote by $\mathcal{L}_f(\theta)$ and $\mathcal{L}_r(\theta)$ the expected losses on a forget set $\mathcal{D}_f$ and a retain set $\mathcal{D}_r$, respectively. Following~\citet{chekalina_2026_gfwsvd}, the empirical Fisher information at $\theta^\star$, restricted to $\mathbf{W}$, admits the Kronecker factorization
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}_F(\mathbf{W}) \approx \mathbf{A} \otimes \mathbf{B},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ and $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ are recovered from gradients $\{\mathbf{G}_i\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{D}|}$ via a truncated SVD of the rearranged Fisher. This is computed in a matrix-free manner using Lanczos iteration~\citep{Lanczos1950} with matrix--vector products of the form
\begin{equation}
    \sum_i \mathbf{G}_i^\top \mathbf{Z} \mathbf{G}_i.
\end{equation}
The matrix-free Fisher factorization (MFF) algorithm reduces the construction cost from $\mathcal{O}(m^2 n^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(mn^2 + m^2n)$, making the procedure tractable for 7B-scale models.

In this work, we run MFF twice per layer: once on $\mathcal{D}_f$ to obtain $\mathbf{A}_f, \mathbf{B}_f$, and once on $\mathcal{D}_r$ to obtain $\mathbf{A}_r, \mathbf{B}_r$. Each factor is regularized using Tikhonov damping, $\mathbf{A} \leftarrow \mathbf{A} + \varepsilon \mathbf{I}$ with $\varepsilon>0$, before Cholesky factorization:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{L}_A \mathbf{L}_A^\top, \qquad \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{L}_B \mathbf{L}_B^\top.
\end{equation}

That ensures all four factors are SPD, that the Cholesky factors $\mathbf{L}_{A_\bullet},\mathbf{L}_{B_\bullet}$ are invertible, and that $\mathbf{K}_A=\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{A_f}$ and $\mathbf{K}_B=\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{B_f}$ are non-singular. In particular $\sigma_{A,i},\sigma_{B,j}>0$ for all $(i,j)$, which we use freely in Section~\ref{sec:two_fisher_gsvd} and Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs}.

\subsection{Two-Fisher Wiener Edit}
\label{sec:two_fisher_gsvd}

We seek a perturbation that removes forget-Fisher mass from the post-edit weight while paying for the edit in the retain metric:
\begin{equation}
    J(\mathbf{\Delta})
    =
    \lVert \mathbf{W}+\mathbf{\Delta} \rVert^2_{F_f}
    +
    \lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}
    \lVert \mathbf{\Delta} \rVert^2_{F_r},
\label{eq:kforge_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\lVert \mathbf{X} \rVert^2_{F_\bullet}
= \operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X})^\top
(\mathbf{A}_\bullet \otimes \mathbf{B}_\bullet)
\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X})$. We define cross-Cholesky maps
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_A = \mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{A_f},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{K}_B = \mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{B_f},
\end{equation}
with SVDs $\mathbf{K}_A = \mathbf{U}_A \mathbf{\Sigma}_A \mathbf{V}_A^\top$ and
$\mathbf{K}_B = \mathbf{U}_B \mathbf{\Sigma}_B \mathbf{V}_B^\top$. The rotated forget-whitened weight is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}
    =
    \mathbf{V}_B^\top
    \mathbf{L}_{B_f}^\top
    \mathbf{W}
    \mathbf{L}_{A_f}
    \mathbf{V}_A.
\end{equation}

Define the simultaneous diagonalizers $\mathbf{Q}_A = \mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_A$ and $\mathbf{Q}_B = \mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_B$, which jointly diagonalize $(\mathbf{A}_f,\mathbf{A}_r)$ and $(\mathbf{B}_f,\mathbf{B}_r)$, and let $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{Q}_B^{-1}\mathbf{\Delta}\,\mathbf{Q}_A^{-\top}$ denote the perturbation in this basis.

\begin{theorem}[Unconstrained K-FORGE Wiener edit]
\label{thm:two_fisher_gsvd}
The unique minimizer of Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_objective} over $\mathbf{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\Delta}^\star
    =
    -\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}
    \mathbf{U}_B
    (\mathbf{G} \odot \mathbf{R})
    \mathbf{U}_A^\top
    \mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1},
\label{eq:kforge_delta}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    G_{ji}
    =
    \frac{\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}}
    {\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}}.
\end{equation}
For $r=\min(m,n)$ the rank constraint is inactive, so $\mathbf{\Delta}^\star$ is also the rank-$r$ minimizer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Exact rank-$r$ solution at zero retain penalty]
\label{prop:rankr_lam0}
At $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}=0$, the rank-$r$ minimizer of Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_objective} is obtained by the rescaling $\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_B\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}_A$, which converts the objective into the unweighted Frobenius problem $\lVert\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{R}\rVert_F^2$. Solving via Eckart--Young, $\mathbf{Y}_r=\operatorname{SVD}_r(-\mathbf{R})$, unscaling $\mathbf{H}_r=\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_r\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$, and mapping back yields
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\Delta}^{\!(\!r\!)\!}_{0}\!=\!\!-\!\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\!\mathbf{U}_B\,\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{R})\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}\,
    \!\mathbf{U}_A^\top\!\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{General case: separable relaxation.}
For $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}>0$ and $r<\min(m,n)$, the rank-$r$ problem becomes a weighted-Frobenius low-rank approximation with non-separable weights $\alpha_{ji}=\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}$, which is NP-hard in general~\citep{DBLP:conf/icml/SrebroJ03}. We adopt the separable relaxation that keeps the Wiener-filtered target but replaces the weights with their separable surrogate $\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2$. Concretely,
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{G}_{ji}
    \!=\!
    \frac{\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2}
    {\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}},\!
    \quad
    \mathbf{Y}^\star
    \!=\!
    -(\!\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}\!\odot\!\mathbf{R}),
\label{eq:relaxation_target}
\end{equation}
followed by $\mathbf{Y}_r\!=\!\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{Y}^\star),\mathbf{H}_r\!=\!\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_r\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$, and
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}
    \!=\!\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_B\,\!
    \mathbf{H}_r\,
    \mathbf{U}_A^\top\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}.
\label{eq:kforge_relaxed_delta}
\end{equation}
(The sign is absorbed into $\mathbf{Y}^\star$.)

Moreover, the relaxation is exact for $r = \min (m,n)$ and $\lambda_{\text{ret}}=0$, we prove that in Proposition~\ref{prop:limit_consistency}.

% \paragraph{Interpretation.}
% The products $\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}$ measure how strongly forget curvature dominates retain curvature along the $(j,i)$ axis of the diagonalized basis. The Wiener gain $G_{ji}=\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}/(\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}})$ is bell-shaped in this product, peaking near $\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}}$ and decaying at both spectral extremes: directions where retain dominates ($\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}\to 0$) receive vanishing edit energy because the forget signal is weak, while directions where forget dominates by a large margin ($\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}\to\infty$) are also damped because the underlying target $M_{ji}=R_{ji}/(\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j})$ shrinks as $1/(\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j})$. The relaxation keeps the dominant filtered structure in the retain-whitened basis without overstating a closed-form optimum for the general weighted-rank-$r$ problem.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The product $\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}$ is the forget-to-retain curvature ratio along coordinate $(j,i)$ of the diagonalized basis. The Wiener gain suppresses coordinates with weak forget signal and also regularizes very large ratios through $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}$. Thus the edit concentrates on directions where forget curvature is visible while retain movement remains controlled. The low-rank truncation keeps only the dominant filtered components, producing a compact initialization edit.


\paragraph{Recovering prior work.}
When all four Fisher factors are restricted to be diagonal, $\mathbf{U}_A$ and $\mathbf{U}_B$ collapse to identity and Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_delta} reduces to per-weight Wiener filtering of the singly-whitened weight matrix. The structure becomes close to Selective Synaptic Dampening~\citep{foster24}, but with a Wiener denominator $\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2\!+\!\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}$ rather than SSD's hard-clipped ratio $\min(\alpha F_f/F_r, 1)$; we use this diagonal limit as a control in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Setting $\mathbf{A}_r\!=\!\mathbf{I}$ and $\mathbf{B}_r\!=\!\mathbf{I}$ removes retain awareness entirely and yields our forget-only ablation.

\subsection{The K-FORGE Algorithm}
\label{sec:kforge_algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{K-FORGE}
\label{alg:kforge}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$, forget/retain sets $\mathcal{D}_f,\mathcal{D}_r$, rank $r$, factor damping $\varepsilon$, retain penalty $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}$, strength $\alpha$
\State $(\mathbf{A}_f,\mathbf{B}_f)\leftarrow \operatorname{MFF}(\mathbf{W},\mathcal{D}_f)$;\quad $(\mathbf{A}_r,\mathbf{B}_r)\leftarrow \operatorname{MFF}(\mathbf{W},\mathcal{D}_r)$
\State Damp factors by $\varepsilon\mathbf{I}$ and compute $\mathbf{L}_{A_\bullet},\mathbf{L}_{B_\bullet}$
\State $\mathbf{K}_A\!\leftarrow\!\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{A_f};\mathbf{K}_B\!\leftarrow\!\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{B_f}$
\State $\operatorname{SVD}(\mathbf{K}_A)\!=\!\mathbf{U}_A\mathbf{\Sigma}_A\mathbf{V}_A^\top;\operatorname{SVD}(\mathbf{K}_B)\!=\!\mathbf{U}_B\mathbf{\Sigma}_B\mathbf{V}_B^\top$
\State $\mathbf{R}\leftarrow \mathbf{V}_B^\top\mathbf{L}_{B_f}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{L}_{A_f}\mathbf{V}_A$
\State $\widetilde{G}_{ji}\leftarrow \sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2/(\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}})$
\State $\mathbf{Y}^\star\leftarrow -(\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}\odot \mathbf{R})$;\quad $\mathbf{Y}_r\leftarrow \operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{Y}^\star)$
\State $\mathbf{H}_r\leftarrow \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_r\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$
\State $\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}\leftarrow \mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_B\mathbf{H}_r\mathbf{U}_A^\top\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}$
\State \Return $\mathbf{W} + \alpha\,\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Factor damping and retain penalty play distinct roles. The factor damping $\varepsilon$ only makes the empirical Fisher factors numerically symmetric positive definite~(SPD) before Cholesky factorization; $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}$ controls the retain budget in Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_objective}, entering only through the Wiener gain $\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}$. The post-hoc strength $\alpha$ remains a safety scalar for the downstream initializer. When $r=\min(m,n)$ or $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}=0$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:kforge} returns the exact minimizer of Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_objective} by Theorem~\ref{thm:two_fisher_gsvd} and Proposition~\ref{prop:rankr_lam0}; otherwise it returns the separable relaxation $\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}$. The procedure is training-free: it requires only calibration forward/backward passes to estimate Fisher factors.

\subsection{K-FORGE as Initialization}
\label{sec:kforge_initialization}

After computing the low-rank edit, we form an initialized checkpoint
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\theta}=\theta^\star + \alpha \mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is a post-hoc strength parameter. We then run an unchanged preference-based unlearning method from $\widehat{\theta}$:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{t+1}
  =
  \theta_t - \eta_t \nabla_\theta
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{unlearn}}(\theta_t),
  \qquad
  \theta_0=\widehat{\theta}.
\end{equation}
In our experiments, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{unlearn}}$ is either NPO~\citep{npo} or SimNPO~\citep{simnpo}. \textbf{K-FORGE }does not change the loss, optimizer, data order, or training budget; it changes only the initial checkpoint. We therefore compare the \textbf{K-FORGE} initialized training against a scratch training.  

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

Our main experiments use Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct. We additionally report a limited Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct sanity check on TOFU \texttt{forget10}. All checkpoints are obtained from the OpenUnlearning reference releases~\citep{openunlearning}.

\subsection{Benchmarks.}

We evaluate on TOFU~\cite{tofu}, using the \texttt{forget10}, \texttt{forget05}, and \texttt{forget01} splits. Lower forget metrics and higher utility indicate stronger unlearning. We report means and standard deviations over three random seeds.

\subsection{Metrics.}

We report several metrics from OpenUnlearning.  \textbf{Model Utility} is a harmonic mean over retained-question answering performance, real-author, and world-fact metrics. If utility metric is high, that means model did not lose its original performance. \textbf{Forget Q/A Probability} is the mean normalized probability assigned to the gold forget-set answers; lower values indicate that the model is less likely to reproduce the forgotten answers. \textbf{Forget Q/A ROUGE} measures ROUGE-L recall between generated answers and the forget-set ground-truth answers, the lower values indicate less lexical recovery of the forgotten content. \textbf{Forget Quality} and \textbf{Forget Truth Ratio} are OpenUnlearning aggregate forgetting diagnostics; higher values indicate better forgetting under the benchmark's calibrated scoring. \textbf{PrivLeak} measures privacy leakage; lower values indicate weaker recoverability under the benchmark attack. \textbf{Extraction Strength} measures exact token-level extractability of forget-set answers; lower values indicate weaker memorized continuation.


\subsection{Baselines.}

\textbf{K-FORGE} is an initializer, so in our comparison in this work we fix the unlearning method and change only the starting checkpoint. We compare against initialized from scratch NPO/SimNPO runs, matched-norm low-rank initialization controls, and diagonal-only Fisher initialization.

The base unlearning methods used are NPO~\citep{npo} and SimNPO~\citep{simnpo} initialized from the same pretrained checkpoint and trained for the same number of steps as \textbf{K-FORGE}-initialized runs. That way we show that \textbf{K-FORGE} changes the early optimization trajectory of an otherwise unchanged preference-based unlearning method.

We also investigate whether \textbf{K-FORGE} works simply because it moves the model before fine-tuning, or because it chooses a better unlearning direction. So we compare \textbf{K-FORGE} against two low-rank edits with the same edited modules, rank, and Frobenius norm as K-FORGE: a random rank-$2$ edit and a rank-$2$ edit along the leading singular directions of the weight matrix of the LLM. Each edited checkpoint is then used to initialize the same NPO or SimNPO training run.

Next, we include a diagonal Fisher and a forget-only variant. The diagonal-only baseline keeps only the main diagonals for all Kronecker factors. That is conceptually close to methods like Selective Synaptic Dampening~\citep{foster24}. The forget-only baseline removes retain-Fisher whitening to check whether it is sufficient by itself. We ablate both baselines as one-shot edits and as downstream initializers.

Concretely, our initializer competitors answer four separate failure modes. \textbf{Random matched} asks whether the downstream optimizer simply benefits from being displaced away from the pretrained point. \textbf{Weight-SVD matched} asks whether a generic spectral low-rank direction, independent of the forget/retain split, is enough. \textbf{Diagonal Fisher} asks whether per-parameter Fisher ratios already contain the useful signal, as in SSD-style methods. \textbf{Forget-only Fisher} asks whether the retain Fisher is unnecessary and forget curvature alone can define a good erasure direction. The empirical answer is different for each: random and weight-SVD are generally weaker; diagonal and forget-only recover part of the gain; the full Kronecker two-Fisher initializer is the most reliable early-step Pareto improvement, especially on \texttt{forget10} and SimNPO.

In this work, we focus on NPO and SimNPO because these preference-based unlearning methods are sensitive to initialization. RMU~\citep{rmu}, GradDiff~\citep{tofu}, and other full unlearning methods are important for the field, but including them would answer a different question: which unlearning method performs best, rather than whether K-FORGE improves a fixed downstream optimizer. We therefore treat these comparisons as outside the scope of the initializer ablation.

\subsection{K-FORGE configuration.}
Unless stated otherwise, we use rank $r=2$, factor damping $10^{-4}$, retain penalty $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}=10^{-2}$, and target the \texttt{mlp.down\_proj} module of the selected layer. We use $B_{\mathrm{cal}}=32$ calibration batches for both forget and retain Fishers. The corrected initializer is the Wiener variant from Section~\ref{sec:method}; the main initialization experiments use strengths $\alpha \in \{0.45, 0.60\}$ and step budgets $N \in \{50,100,250\}$. For the post-hoc placement and strength audit in Section~\ref{sec:layer_strength_audit}, we keep the downstream optimizer fixed to NPO at $N=50$ and sweep only the edited layer or scalar strength.

\subsection{K-FORGE compute overhead.}
The proposed method poses additional computation overhead due to the need for gradient collection on the calibration data, which we study and discuss in this section. 

First, the proposed method is a one-time overhead before downstream unlearning. We estimate forget and retain Fisher factors on calibration batches (32 in this work). Then we compute the Cholesky/SVD components of the edit, and apply the resulting low-rank update. This computational overhead is done only once per run and does not change the per-step cost of the subsequent NPO or SimNPO runs. 

We report measured wall-clock time from saved the method runs together with FLOPs estimates for the same unlearning configurations in Table~\ref{tab:kforge_compute}. For the 1B and 3B settings used in this work, the full initialization pass is about 9--11\% of a representative 50-step scratch unlearning run. 

To sum up, the proposed method is not free in terms of compute, but its overhead is relatively small compared even to the shortest downstream budgets we evaluate.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work we proposed \textbf{K-FORGE}, a Fisher information-based initializer for iterative unlearning methods. We prove that our method yields an exact full-rank solution and low-rank relaxation. Through extensive experiments we show that the proposed approach is an effective one-shot initialization method for NPO and SimNPO unlearning methods for different on TOFU subsets.


\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our work has several limitations which we acknowledge below. 

First, \textbf{K-FORGE} is not a standalone unlearning method. It is complementary to existing iterative unlearning methods like NPO, SimNPO, but not a replacement for them.  

Next, while our experiments are extensive, we still test only two models of relatively moderate size of 1B and 3B parameters across different (\texttt{forget10}, \texttt{forget05}, \texttt{forget01}) subsets of one benchmark TOFU. We therefore do not claim transfer to MUSE or other benchmarks, robustness against quantization revert, or broad large-model scaling. Our relearning audit is also limited to one epoch of direct supervised fine-tuning on \texttt{forget10}; stronger or longer recovery attacks may behave differently. 

The method also uses the collection of gradients using calibration data and several dense factorizations, which are affordable at 1B--3B scale but might become challenging and pose an additional computational cost relative to scratch fine-tuning.

\section{Proofs}
\label{sec:proofs}

Throughout this appendix we use the notation of Section~\ref{sec:method}. Let $\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$, $\mathbf{A}_\bullet\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$, $\mathbf{B}_\bullet\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$, with Cholesky factors $\mathbf{L}_{A_\bullet}$, $\mathbf{L}_{B_\bullet}$ and cross-Cholesky SVDs
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{K}_A &= \mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{A_f} = \mathbf{U}_A\mathbf{\Sigma}_A\mathbf{V}_A^\top, \\
\mathbf{K}_B &= \mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{B_f} = \mathbf{U}_B\mathbf{\Sigma}_B\mathbf{V}_B^\top.
\end{align*}
The simultaneous diagonalizers are $\mathbf{Q}_A = \mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_A$ and $\mathbf{Q}_B = \mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_B$. Standard algebra gives $\mathbf{Q}_A^\top\mathbf{A}_f\mathbf{Q}_A = \mathbf{\Sigma}_A^2$, $\mathbf{Q}_A^\top\mathbf{A}_r\mathbf{Q}_A = \mathbf{I}$, and analogously for $\mathbf{Q}_B$. We parametrize the perturbation as $\mathbf{\Delta} = \mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Q}_A^\top$ with $\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$.

\subsection{Lemma: change of coordinates}
\label{app:coordlemma}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:coords}
Let $\mathbf{M} := \mathbf{Q}_B^{-1}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Q}_A^{-\top}$. Then
\[
J(\mathbf{\Delta}) = \sum_{j,i}\Big[\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2(M_{ji}+H_{ji})^2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{ret}} H_{ji}^2\Big],
\]
and $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$, where $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{V}_B^\top\mathbf{L}_{B_f}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{L}_{A_f}\mathbf{V}_A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition of $\mathbf{M}$, $\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{M}\mathbf{Q}_A^\top$, so $\mathbf{W}+\mathbf{\Delta} = \mathbf{Q}_B(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{H})\mathbf{Q}_A^\top$. Using $\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Q}_A^\top) = (\mathbf{Q}_A\otimes\mathbf{Q}_B)\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X})$ and the diagonalizing identities,
\begin{equation}
\lVert\mathbf{W}+\mathbf{\Delta}\rVert^2_{F_f}=
\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{H})^\top
\bigl(\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^2\otimes\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^2\bigr)\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{H}) =\sum_{j,i}
\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2
(M_{ji}+H_{ji})^2 .
\end{equation}
and analogously $\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}\rVert^2_{F_r} = \lVert\mathbf{H}\rVert_F^2$.

For the second claim, $\mathbf{K}_B = \mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-1}\mathbf{L}_{B_f}$ gives $\mathbf{L}_{B_r}\mathbf{U}_B = \mathbf{L}_{B_f}\mathbf{V}_B\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}$, hence $\mathbf{L}_{B_r} = \mathbf{L}_{B_f}\mathbf{V}_B\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{U}_B^\top$ and $\mathbf{U}_B^\top\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^\top = \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{V}_B^\top\mathbf{L}_{B_f}^\top$. By analogous algebra, $\mathbf{L}_{A_r}\mathbf{U}_A = \mathbf{L}_{A_f}\mathbf{V}_A\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{M} &= \mathbf{Q}_B^{-1}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Q}_A^{-\top} = \mathbf{U}_B^\top\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{L}_{A_r}\mathbf{U}_A \\
&= \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{V}_B^\top\mathbf{L}_{B_f}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{L}_{A_f}\mathbf{V}_A\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1} \\
&= \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}. \qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two_fisher_gsvd}}
\label{app:thm_proof}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:coords}, $J$ is separable across entries $(j,i)$, so its Hessian in $\mathbf{H}$ is diagonal with entries $2(\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{ret}})$. Under the assumption $\sigma_{A,i},\sigma_{B,j}>0$ (Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}), each entry is strictly positive, so $J$ is strictly convex and admits a unique minimizer. Setting the partial derivative to zero,
\[
2\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2(M_{ji}+H_{ji}) + 2\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}H_{ji} = 0,
\]
yields
\[
H_{ji}^\star = -\frac{\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2}{\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}}\,M_{ji}.
\]
Substituting $M_{ji} = R_{ji}/(\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j})$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:coords},
\[
H_{ji}^\star = -\frac{\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}}{\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}}\,R_{ji} = -G_{ji}R_{ji},
\]
so $\mathbf{H}^\star = -(\mathbf{G}\odot\mathbf{R})$. Mapping back,
\[
\mathbf{\Delta}^\star = \mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{H}^\star\mathbf{Q}_A^\top = -\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_B(\mathbf{G}\odot\mathbf{R})\mathbf{U}_A^\top\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}.
\]
For $r=\min(m,n)$ the rank constraint is inactive, so $\mathbf{\Delta}^\star$ is also the rank-$r$ minimizer.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rankr_lam0}}
\label{app:prop1_proof}

\begin{proof}
Set $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}=0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:coords},
\[
J(\mathbf{\Delta})\big|_{\lambda=0}
= \sum_{j,i}\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2(M_{ji}+H_{ji})^2
= \lVert\mathbf{\Sigma}_B(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{H})\mathbf{\Sigma}_A\rVert_F^2.
\]


Using $\mathbf{\Sigma}_B\mathbf{M}\mathbf{\Sigma}_A = \mathbf{R}$ and defining $\mathbf{Y} := \mathbf{\Sigma}_B\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}_A$,
\[
J|_{\lambda=0}(\mathbf{H}) = \lVert\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{R}\rVert_F^2.
\]
Since $\mathbf{\Sigma}_A,\mathbf{\Sigma}_B$ are invertible diagonal and $\mathbf{Q}_A,\mathbf{Q}_B$ are invertible, $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{\Delta})\le r \Leftrightarrow \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{H})\le r \Leftrightarrow \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{Y})\le r$. The constrained problem becomes
\[
\min_{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{Y})\le r}\lVert\mathbf{Y} - (-\mathbf{R})\rVert_F^2,
\]
whose Eckart--Young (Frobenius) minimizer is $\mathbf{Y}_r = \operatorname{SVD}_r(-\mathbf{R}) = -\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{R})$ (unique when $\sigma_r(\mathbf{R}) > \sigma_{r+1}(\mathbf{R})$; otherwise any rank-$r$ truncation attains the optimum). Unscaling, $\mathbf{H}_r = \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_r\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1} = -\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{R})\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$, and
\[
\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}_{\lambda=0} = \mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{H}_r\mathbf{Q}_A^\top = -\mathbf{L}_{B_r}^{-\top}\mathbf{U}_B\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{R})\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}\mathbf{U}_A^\top\mathbf{L}_{A_r}^{-1}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:limit_consistency}: Limit consistency of the relaxation.}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:limit_consistency}
The relaxed edit $\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_relaxed_delta} coincides with the exact minimizer of Eq.~\ref{eq:kforge_objective} in two limits: (i) at $r=\min(m,n)$ it equals $\mathbf{\Delta}^\star$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:two_fisher_gsvd} for any $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}\ge 0$; (ii) at $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}=0$ it equals the rank-$r$ minimizer from Proposition~\ref{prop:rankr_lam0} for any $r$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Case (i): $r = \min(m,n)$.} The truncation $\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{Y}^\star) = \mathbf{Y}^\star$ is vacuous, so $\mathbf{H}_r = \mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\mathbf{Y}^\star\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1} = -\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}\odot\mathbf{R})\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$. Entrywise,
\[
(H_r)_{ji} = -\frac{\widetilde{G}_{ji}}{\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}}R_{ji}
= -\frac{\sigma_{A,i}\sigma_{B,j}}{\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}}}R_{ji}
= -G_{ji}R_{ji},
\]
which is $\mathbf{H}^\star$ from the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two_fisher_gsvd}. Hence $\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)} = \mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{H}^\star\mathbf{Q}_A^\top = \mathbf{\Delta}^\star$.

\emph{Case (ii): $\lambda_{\mathrm{ret}} = 0$.} Then 


$$  
\widetilde{G}_{ji} = \frac{\sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2}{ \sigma_{A,i}^2\sigma_{B,j}^2} = 1.
$$ 

So $\mathbf{Y}^\star = -\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{Y}_r = \operatorname{SVD}_r(-\mathbf{R})$. 

Then $$\mathbf{H}_r = -\mathbf{\Sigma}_B^{-1}\operatorname{SVD}_r(\mathbf{R})\mathbf{\Sigma}_A^{-1}$$ and $$\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)} = \mathbf{Q}_B\mathbf{H}_r\mathbf{Q}_A^\top$$ matches $\mathbf{\Delta}^{(r)}_{\lambda=0}$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:rankr_lam0} exactly.
\end{proof}
